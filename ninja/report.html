The score rates one claim from the CV, not the person. It weighs ownership heaviest and communication lightest: overstating “led the migration” costs a candidate far more here than plain English does. Confidence says how much fifteen minutes could establish at all — at L we are not allowed to write FIT or a score above 60.

Why NARROW

Real operational experience, but not at the level the job description assumes. Hire him to run and extend an existing event pipeline and he will do well. Hire him to design your streaming architecture from scratch and you are betting on something he has not actually done.

What we probed

The CV claims: “Led the migration to an event-driven architecture, Kafka, 40M events/day.” That is the largest claim on the page, so the whole 15 minutes went into it. No puzzles, no trivia — one claim, pushed until it either held or stopped holding.

Three follow-ups did the work: what broke in production, why Kafka rather than the alternatives, and who made the call.

Every quote below carries a timecode into the call transcript. A report without quotes is not a report — we reject it and do not pay for it.

Ratings

Technical depth 3 / 4

Strong where he actually lived. Described consumer lag during a partition rebalance in detail — the symptom, how they spotted it, the retry storm it caused downstream, and the fix. That is not something you can read up on.

07:12 — “We had a consumer group that took nine minutes to rebalance and every rebalance replayed about four minutes of traffic. The fix wasn't clever — we just stopped putting slow HTTP calls inside the consumer.”
Problem solving 2 / 4

Weakest axis. Asked why Kafka rather than SQS or Postgres-backed queues at their volume, he gave the marketing answer — throughput and durability — and could not describe the trade-off in his own terms or say what they gave up. When pushed on what he would do differently, he restated the same architecture.

11:40 — “Honestly the decision was made before I joined the team. I inherited it.” — volunteered, not extracted. Credit for that.
Communication 4 / 4

Explains cleanly, distinguishes what he knows from what he assumes, and says “I don't know” without padding. Would be reliable in an incident review and safe in front of a customer.

Ownership 2 / 4

The gap between the CV and the conversation sits here. “Led the migration” turned out to mean he owned the consumer side of an already-decided migration and ran it well. The architecture, the vendor call and the rollout plan belonged to a staff engineer who has since left.

Flags

  • “Led” is doing heavy lifting on the CV. He did not oversell it in conversation — he corrected it himself within two questions — but the written claim would mislead anyone who did not ask.
  • No design experience under constraint. Everything he described was executed inside a decision someone else made.
  • Single-employer depth. Four years, one stack, one scale. Not disqualifying, but he has not had to be wrong about a system yet.

If you proceed

  • Give him a design exercise with a real constraint — “this must survive a 6-hour downstream outage, and you cannot add a new datastore.” His reasoning under constraint is the open question, and 15 minutes could not close it.
  • Ask him to name a technical decision he argued against and lost, and what he thinks now. His answer to ownership questions will either hold or repeat.
  • Check the reference on the staff engineer who left, not on his manager. The manager saw delivery; that engineer saw judgement.
  • If the role is “extend and operate”, most of the above is irrelevant and he is a good hire. Re-read the job description before you decide.

What this assessment cannot tell you

  • Fifteen minutes tests one claim deeply, not breadth. Everything else on his CV is untested.
  • We do not assess culture fit, motivation, or whether he will stay. We were not in the room for that.
  • We did not see him write code. This is about depth of experience, not typing.
  • We take a transcript of the call, never a recording, and delete it 30 days after delivery. The quotes above stay yours.
  • A NARROW verdict is not a rejection. It means the evidence supports a tighter role than the one advertised.
  • We do not score IQ, culture fit, or whether someone will stay. None of that is measurable here, so none of it is in the number.
  • We publish no market medians until we have twenty assessments in the same stack. Percentiles on a small sample are false precision.
